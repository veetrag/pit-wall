# V2 Roadmap — decomposition and open decisions

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Status:** Planning. Nothing here is approved; every section ends in a decision that is Veet's.

This is not a spec. It decomposes the remaining V2 items into independent
sub-projects, records what each one actually costs given the code as it stands
today, and lists the questions that must be answered before any of them can be
specced. Each sub-project gets its own spec -> plan -> implementation cycle.

---

## 0. Two facts that drive the sequencing

Both were found by reading the code, and both change what "do this later" costs.

### 0.1 Bumping the save version silently deletes everyone's season

`SeasonSnapshotSchema` pins `v: z.literal(SAVE_VERSION)`
(`src/state/seasonPersistence.ts:98`). A snapshot whose version does not match
exactly fails `safeParse`, and the loader's response is to discard it:

```
console.warn('[season] discarding unreadable save', ...)
return null
```

There is no migration path. So ANY V2 feature that adds a field to the save --
which is most of them -- wipes every in-progress season on the device it lands on.

Before launch that costs nothing. After launch it is a player losing a
twelve-race championship to an app update.

**This makes save-shape work a gate, not a detail.** Either the schema-changing
V2 items land before the store listing, or a migration layer is built first.
Deciding that is the first decision below, because it constrains the order of
everything else.

### 0.2 There is no career today -- `playAgain` is a factory reset

At the end of 12 races the season moves to `championship`, and the only exit is
`playAgain()` (`src/state/seasonStore.ts`), which sets:

```
playerDriverId: null, selectedTeamId: null, raceIndex: 0, standings: [],
devPoints: 0, purchasedTiers: {}, rivalDevelopment: {}
```

Everything the player built is discarded. The upgrades they spent twelve races
earning, the team they chose, the championship they won -- all gone, back to
the menu as if the app were freshly installed.

So "multi-season career" is not an extension of the season wrapper. The season
wrapper is complete and self-contained; a career is a NEW layer above it that
owns what survives a reset. That is why it is sub-project A below and why
everything else is cheaper.

## A. Multi-season career

**What exists:** a complete single season -- 12 rounds, standings, development
points, rival development, a championship screen.

**What is missing:** a layer above the season that persists across one, and a
reason to keep playing after the trophy.

**The work, once the questions are answered:**

1. A `careerStore` (or a career slice) holding what outlives a season, saved
   separately from the season snapshot so an abandoned season does not cost the
   career.
2. `playAgain` split into two actions: "new season, same career" and "abandon
   career". Today's single reset is the second one, mislabelled.
3. A season-number concept. `currentSeed()` is
   `raceIndex * 1000 + 42 + attemptNonce * 7919` and has no season term, so
   season 2 replays season 1's weather and races exactly. That is a one-line
   change but it MUST be made deliberately -- see the open question on whether
   replaying a known season is a feature.
4. Rival development carried forward, so the grid the player beat last year is
   stronger this year. `rivalDevelopment` already accumulates per-race; it is
   currently thrown away at season end.
5. Save schema change -> see section 0.1.

**Open questions, all Veet's:**

- **A1. What carries over?** The credible options are very different games.
  *Everything* (upgrades persist, the player snowballs and season 3 is a
  victory lap); *nothing but reputation* (a clean slate each year, upgrades are
  a within-season economy, closest to how the game plays now); or *the car
  carries, the driver contract does not* (the F1-authentic answer, and the only
  one where changing teams means something).
- **A2. Does the player keep their seat?** If a career spans seasons, either the
  player is offered contracts based on results -- which is a whole new system
  and the main reason careers are compelling -- or the seat is fixed and the
  career is just a longer number.
- **A3. Do rivals get stronger every season?** Without it, season 4 is easier
  than season 1 because only the player has accumulated. With it, the game
  needs a difficulty ceiling or it becomes unwinnable.
- **A4. How does a career end?** Retirement after N seasons, an endless run, or
  a goal ("win three titles")? An endless career with no arc is where most
  management games lose players.

**Depends on:** the 0.1 decision. Nothing else.
**Risk:** highest of the four. A1 and A2 together decide what the game IS after
race 12, and they cannot be deferred to implementation.

## B. Practice session -- CUT

**Decision (Veet, 2026-09-19): practice is not in V2.**

The reasoning, which is the right one: practice exists to dial in setup, and
there is no setup to dial. Without aerodynamic components on the car that the
player can actually adjust for a circuit, a practice session has nothing to do.

The code agrees. `aero` exists, but as a permanent TEAM FACET
(`src/data/upgrades.json`: `aero` / `team`, alongside `power`, `handling`,
`reliability`, and `pitCrew` on the car). It is a capability number bought with
development points across a season -- not a per-weekend tunable. There is no
wing angle, no downforce level, and no circuit-specific optimum for a session
to search for. A practice session against today's model would be a screen that
consumes time and returns nothing the player can act on.

This also retires the weaker jobs §B previously floated. "Information" was
always thin -- the tyre cliff gauge and the pit projection already tell the
player most of what practice would reveal, so it would have been a loading
screen with extra steps. "Tyre allocation" was never a job of its own; it is C.

**What would reopen this:** a per-weekend car setup model -- a real trade-off
the player sets before running (downforce traded against straight-line speed is
the obvious one) with a circuit-dependent optimum. That is a change to the lap
model, not a screen, and it is a bigger piece of work than the practice session
it would justify. If that is ever wanted, it should be brainstormed on its own
terms as "car setup", and practice reconsidered afterwards as the session that
serves it. Not the other way round.

## C. Weekend-spanning tyre allocation

**What exists:** `tyreAllocationBase` per difficulty in `tuning.json`,
`consumeTyreSet`, and `UNLIMITED_TYRE_ALLOCATION`. Allocation is already
modelled -- it is just scoped to a single race.

**What is missing:** the scope change, and the sessions that would spend from
the shared pool.

This is the smallest of the four by a wide margin. The engine work is mostly
moving an existing structure up a level.

**The work:**

1. Allocation moves from race-scoped to weekend-scoped state.
2. Qualifying spends from it. It currently does not -- deliberately excluded
   from the qualifying spec (section 2, "No tyre-set consumption") precisely
   because this item was deferred.
3. The race inherits what is left.
4. Save schema change -> section 0.1.

**Open questions:**

- **C1. Is the constraint fun?** Real F1 tyre allocation is a bookkeeping
  burden that produces a handful of dramatic moments per season. The same could
  be true here, or it could just make qualifying worse by punishing the player
  for engaging with it.
- **C2. Does it invalidate the qualifying design?** Qualifying currently has no
  carry-over cost, which is why the compound choice reads as "how much grip do
  you want" rather than a trap. Making it consume sets changes that decision --
  possibly for the better (it becomes a real trade-off), possibly into the
  "obvious answer" problem the qualifying spec explicitly avoided.

**Depends on:** nothing technically. Conceptually it reopens a settled
qualifying decision, so it should not land while qualifying is still being
judged on device.
**Risk:** low technically, moderate in design -- it can make a working feature
worse.

## D. Monetisation

**What exists:** nothing. No ads, no IAP, no analytics, no store listing.

**What is missing:** the decision. Everything else follows from it.

This is listed last deliberately: it is the only item that changes what the
other three are FOR. A game with a paid unlock wants a demo boundary; a game
with ads wants session-length pacing; a premium game wants none of that and
wants polish instead.

**Open questions:**

- **D1. Which model?** Premium (one price, no ads -- fits the "broadcast-grade,
  no compromise" design language), free with ads, or free with a paid unlock
  after N races.
- **D2. If not premium, where does the boundary go?** "First season free" is
  the obvious one and interacts directly with A.
- **D3. Analytics at all?** Any analytics is a privacy decision and a store
  disclosure, and the game currently collects nothing. That is a genuine
  feature, and worth keeping unless there is a reason not to.

**Depends on:** nothing. Blocks the store listing.
**Risk:** low to build, high to get wrong. Also the only item with a
non-engineering deadline attached to it.

## Suggested order, and why

1. **Decide 0.1** (migrate vs land-before-launch). One decision, gates the rest.
2. **Answer A1 and A2.** They define the game after race 12. Until they are
   answered, C is being designed for a game whose shape is unknown.
3. **C**, if it survives C2. Smallest, and it should land before qualifying's
   numbers are locked in players' hands rather than after.
4. **A.** The largest, and the one that makes the game a game rather than a
   twelve-race demo.
5. **D** before the store listing, independent of the rest.

B is cut (see above), so V2 is three items, not four. The instinct it was
guarding against still stands generally: the cheapest thing to build is not
the same as the thing most worth building, and resembling an existing pattern
is not a reason to build something.

## Not V2, but adjacent and cheap

Carried from the working to-do list so they are not lost:

- `projectPitNow` is seeded with `Date.now() % 100000` (`raceStore.ts:201`), so
  the same race state gives different pit advice each time the panel opens. The
  only number in the game not reproducible from a seed, and now conspicuous
  next to qualifying's strict determinism.
- Round one of every new season is WET. `currentSeed()` at `raceIndex 0`,
  `attemptNonce 0` is 42, which is wet at bengaluru. A new player's first ever
  race is the punishing one. One-line change if that is not wanted.
- ~20 literal `rgba` triples across 12 files that the `--accent-rgb` channel
  token pattern would replace.
